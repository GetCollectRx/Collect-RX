## 1. What I verified (with quotes as returned by search)

### 1.1 Storage and tow disputes with insurers exist and reach court (SECONDARY)

- Intact v. Vaughan towing operator, Ontario Superior Court, May 2026. URL: https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/legal-insights/ontario-court-rules-towing-firm-overcharged-intact-as-dispute-hits-483k-574766.aspx (Insurance Business Canada). Title: "Ontario court rules towing firm overcharged Intact as dispute hits $483K". Excerpt: "a decision filed on May 8, 2026, the Ontario Superior Court of Justice sided with Intact Insurance Company in a fight over how much a Vaughan-area towing operator could charge for storing vehicles." The summary says Vaughan By-Law 122-2022 capped storage at $55/day outdoor and $60/day indoor, and cites 2026 ONSC 2578 (case number as reported by the summary, unchecked).
- Intact repeated court applications. URL: https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/legal/how-intact-lost-this-battle-over-vehicle-storage-fees (Canadian Underwriter). Summary: from 2012 through 2018 Intact made 108 applications under s. 24 of the Repair and Storage Liens Act against one towing company, and a 2019 Superior Court ruling applied a $70/day rate. Summary of a 2016 Small Claims ruling: "In 2016, a Small Claims Court judge ruled in favour of J.P. Towing over the $70 daily storage rate that the towing company charged".
- Insurer standing to dispute storage: 409 Collision Centre v. Intact (2016 ONSC 7711; 2018 ONCA 394), via Harper Grey and Canadian Underwriter summaries (https://insurancelawblog.harpergrey.com/?p=17874). Summary: the Court of Appeal reasoned "that requiring insurers to settle everything first would let storage charges pile up". The insured must object within three days of an initial certificate or release the vehicle (vLex summary, https://vlex.com/vid/ontario-court-of-appeal-715668697).
- Vehicles held during price negotiation. URL: https://insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/auto/manager-hits-out-at-towing-fees-that-are-deterring-insurers-62835.aspx (Insurance Business Canada, old, date not shown). Excerpt: "While negotiating with insurers, the towing companies will hold the automobiles in storage." One dispute reportedly took about two weeks. OPINION from a dealership manager, reported in trade press.
- Trade body view: Provincial Towing Association of Ontario president Joey Gagne "commented that towing companies should be issuing bills that have full breakdowns of any related costs under Ontario's Consumer Protection Act." (same URL; SECONDARY).

### 1.2 Statute and regulation (PRIMARY, but only listing-level text seen)

- Towing and Storage Safety and Enforcement Act, 2021 and O. Reg. 162/23 (Charges for Towing and Vehicle Storage Services). URLs: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/230162/v4 and https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/21t26/v8. Search summary: contents cover "the form and updates of the rate schedule, towing and storage charges, 'unreasonable amount' criteria and notice, and payment". The Act has sections on insurance, estimates, invoices and charges. I could not read the text.
- Canadian Underwriter (April 2026): operators must publish maximum rates with MTO and cannot charge above them; an insurance executive said the regime has not lowered claims costs because Ontario has not capped tow fees. URL: https://canadianunderwriter.ca/news/claims/why-ontarios-new-tow-truck-regulations-arent-reducing-insurers-costs/ (SECONDARY).
- Repair and Storage Liens Act notice period: reduced from 60 days to 15 days for Ontario vehicles effective July 1, 2016. Excerpt: "the notice period is reduced from 60 days to 15 days after the day after the vehicle is received." Also "a storer's lien is limited to the unpaid amount owing for that period" if notice is not given. URL: https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/insurance/new-ontario-vehicle-storage-lien-rules-take-effect-july-1 (SECONDARY). The 2017 amendments were framed as removing "associated costs from the auto insurance system".

## 4. Conflicting evidence

- Tow storage: 2019 ruling upheld $70/day for Toronto firm; May 2026 ruling enforced $55/$60 Vaughan municipal caps. Rates depend on municipality and contract, so there is no single rate.
- Tow rate regime: Ontario now requires operators to publish maximum rates (2024), while Canadian Underwriter (April 2026) says it has not lowered insurer costs. Unclear whether rate disputes shrink or persist.
- Software: job posts list Mitchell, ARMS, Collision Link, Progi, which suggests shops already have systems that track supplements. Whether staff still call is unproven. Vendors market "virtually automatic" status updates (CCC Update Plus, 2012, old and US) which would reduce call need.
- Most pain evidence is US (BodyShop Business, FenderBender, Repairer Driven News). Ontario DRP and tort/no fault structure differ, so transfer is unproven.
- Who pays after authorization: court reasoning (insurer can seek certificate) conflicts with the intuition that the owner always owes; not resolved.

## 5. Buyer objections found

- Recording and consent: vendor blogs say Canadian privacy law (PIPEDA) requires meaningful consent and disclosure that callers speak to an automated assistant; Quebec Law 25 stricter. SECONDARY vendor blogs (callin.io, lethub.co, oncehub), unverified against OPC.
- Insurer relationship: 2013 Hansard says shops feared insurer retaliation. An AI caller that annoys an adjuster could harm a DRP relationship. This is a plausible objection with weak direct evidence.
- Trust and fraud scrutiny: Aviva's undercover audit shows insurers audit shop billing; shops may not want third parties speaking on bills. Inference, labelled as such.
- Accuracy: wrong statements about supplements or storage could create liability. NO EVIDENCE FOUND in sources.
