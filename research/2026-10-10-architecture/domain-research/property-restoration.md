## A. What I verified (sourced, with tags)

### A1. Ontario Construction Act (prompt payment, liens)
- Owner must pay within 28 days of receiving a proper invoice unless it serves a notice of non-payment within 14 days; undisputed portion still due in 28 days. SECONDARY. https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/Updates-to-Prompt-Payment-Legislation and https://www.nihanglaw.ca/blog-ontario-construction-act-2026-homeowner-guide/
- Contractor must pay subcontractors within 7 days of receiving payment from the owner. SECONDARY (same sources).
- A 2026 change (effective Jan 1, 2026 per one law firm) deems an invoice proper if the owner does not object in writing within 7 days. SECONDARY, single source (Nihang Law), cites O. Reg. 264/25 and Bill 60 (2025). Exact wording and date UNVERIFIED. https://www.nihanglaw.ca/blog-ontario-construction-act-2026-homeowner-guide/
- Lien preservation generally 60 days from substantial performance/completion/termination; perfection 90 days after the last day to preserve. SECONDARY (s. 31 and s. 36 as cited by the same firm). Reconfirm against https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c30 (statute page exists; I could not open it).
- Holdback provisions sit in Part IV from s. 22; owner trust in s. 7. SECONDARY (table-of-contents pages surfaced in search: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c30/v19). The 10 percent holdback figure is general knowledge, UNVERIFIED here.
- Whether and how the Act applies when an insurer, not the owner, funds repairs: NOT FOUND. UNVERIFIED. The contract is normally between owner and contractor, so the owner is the "owner" under the Act (inference, section B).

### A2. Insurer response and payment rules
- Fire statutory conditions: insurer pays within 60 days after proof of loss received, or within 15 days after a dispute-resolution decision. SECONDARY and the sources were not clearly Ontario (glossary and a Canadian policy form). Treat as UNVERIFIED for Ontario until Insurance Act, R.S.O. 1990 c. I.8 and the statutory conditions are read. https://www.lawinsider.com/dictionary/statutory-condition
- Mortgage clause supersedes conflicting policy conditions to the mortgagee's interest: Royal Bank of Canada v. State Farm (SCC 2005). SECONDARY. https://insurancelawblog.harpergrey.com/?p=11445
- Limitation period: the one-year action bar in fire statutory conditions is contested; Limitations Act, 2002 two years may govern. SECONDARY, unsettled. https://mondaq.com/canada/insurance-laws-and-products/692350/your-clients-house-burned-down-more-than-a-year-ago-can-her-insurer-rely-on-the-one-year-limitation-period-in-her-home-insurance-policy
- FSRA Home Insurance Thematic Review (June 2024): 20 insurers, 97 percent of market by 2021 premiums; average 127 days to settle a home claim (range 47 to 180; a later figure of 123 also reported); five findings including inconsistent proof of loss use, no demonstrated oversight of independent adjusters, inconsistent consumer communication; data self-reported, not verified by FSRA. SECONDARY-high (FSRA report surfaced in search but not opened; trade press confirms). https://www.fsrao.ca/media/25821/download and https://canadianunderwriter.ca/2024/06/26/are-home-insurers-properly-overseeing-independent-adjusters-fsra-report/

- FSRA temporary catastrophe measures (March 2025 ice storm, to July 4, 2025) let out-of-province adjusters work in Ontario; current status unknown. SECONDARY. https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/catastrophe/fsra-eases-rules-after-ontario-ice-storm-532589.aspx

## B. What I inferred (UNVERIFIED unless noted)

Answers to the numbered questions, built on section A plus industry reasoning.

### 1. Debtor, payer, administrator, cheque, documents
- Independent contractor: the legal debtor is normally the owner/insured under the repair contract (consistent with A3 "works on the insured's behalf"). The insurer owes the insured under the policy, not the contractor, unless a direction to pay or assignment exists, or the insurer separately contracts with the vendor. Cheque usually to insured and mortgagee, sometimes plus contractor via direction to pay. Documents: work authorization, direction to pay, certificate of completion, sometimes mortgagee consent.
- Franchise or insurer preferred-vendor program: may be a direct vendor relationship with insurer or TPA (service level agreement per A3), with insurer-set pricing and direct payment, while the owner still owes deductible. Terms differ per program: UNVERIFIED, must be confirmed per client.
- Accommodation: the insurer or its adjuster authorizes ALE; provider may bill the insurer directly (if authorized) or the insured pays and is reimbursed. Debtor depends on the authorization.
- Administrator: either the insurer's staff adjuster, an independent adjusting firm, or a TPA. FSRA notes insurers outsource to independent adjusters (A2).

### 2. How status is found, and where phone is the bottleneck
- Likely non-phone: vendor-network platforms (XactAnalysis style) for assignments, estimate upload, status; email to adjuster; accounts payable remittance notices. Where a client is in such a network, phone adds less value.
- Phone is likely the bottleneck for independent contractors outside any network, for "was the cheque issued, to whom, when, and was it mailed or EFT", for adjuster reassignment, and for AP queries at small insurers or adjusting firms. This is inference; no source quantifies it. Validate by asking prospective customers for their last 20 stalled invoices and how each was resolved.

### 3. Delay causes: what a call can do
- Call can resolve (information only): status of file, whether estimate or invoice was received, whether approved, payment issued date and method and payee names, which document is missing, which adjuster is assigned, whether a supplement was received.
- Needs staff negotiation: scope disputes, supplement approval, pricing disagreement, depreciation/recoverable holdback release conditions, mortgagee endorsement chase, cheque issued to insured only, estimate versus invoice mismatch, TPA approval escalations, ALE extension.
- Legal, never automated: lien preservation or perfection, notices under the Construction Act (notice of non-payment, proper invoice disputes), demand letters, limitation periods, coverage position or denial arguments, bad faith allegations, assignment enforceability. The agent must not state legal positions or deadlines to a counterparty.

### 4. Timelines
- Construction Act figures in A1 apply between owner and contractor. When the insurer is paying the owner, the owner's 28-day clock plausibly runs from proper invoice regardless of whether insurer funds have arrived (inference; verify with counsel). Insurer's own obligation: statutory conditions (60 days from proof of loss, A2, unverified for Ontario) and general good-faith duties. No FSRA numeric clock found.
- Other provinces not researched; do not extrapolate.

### 5. Contact patterns
No source found on voicemail rates, callback behaviour, after-hours or direct lines. All UNVERIFIED. Plausible structure: adjuster direct line/mobile, claims-unit IVR, adjusting firm intake, AP department. Adjuster is often field-based, so voicemail is probably high (hypothesis to measure). Disclosure to a contractor generally needs the insured's authorization (A6, dated); the contractor holding a signed direction to pay or work authorization is the likely basis. Agent should state the authorization on file and accept refusal.

### 6. Identifiers
Claim number, policy number, insured name, loss date, loss address, adjuster name and firm, insurer or TPA name, contractor job/work order number, estimate and invoice numbers and amounts, assignment/authorization number (vendor program), mortgagee name, for accommodation the ALE authorization number, stay dates, nightly rate, confirmation number. List is inference from general practice; field names vary per insurer.

### 7. Reconciliation
Plausible: initial cheque to insured (ACV), staged progress payments, depreciation holdback released on completion proof, supplements paid separately, one claim funding mitigation, rebuild and ALE invoices from different vendors, owner pays deductible to contractor. Agent output should record payee, amount, method, date, per-invoice allocation. UNVERIFIED as to Ontario norms.

### 8. Accommodation charges
Authorization period, nightly cap, approved versus billed nights, extension approval. No source found; only that caps exist as a percent of dwelling coverage (A5). Treat as per-insurer; the agent should capture authorized nights and rate, and flag any variance for staff.

## C. Conclusion

### Distinct call objectives
1. Invoice receipt and status (received, in review, approved, scheduled).
2. Payment trace: issued, date, method, payee(s), cheque/EFT reference, mailing address.
3. Missing document request: what is needed (completion certificate, photos, signed authorization, proof of loss).
4. Adjuster identification or reassignment, and best callback route.
5. Resubmission channel and portal/email address confirmation.
6. Supplement or estimate status (information only).
7. Accommodation: authorization number, authorized nights, rate, extension status, payment status.
8. Escalation requests: promised callback, supervisor, adjuster manager.

### Separate workflows?
Restoration and accommodation should be separate workflows: different identifiers (job and invoice versus authorization and nights), different payers/approvers, and accommodation is time-based with approved-versus-billed variance logic. Restoration has progress/supplement/holdback complexity. Auto rehab differs in payer type (auto insurers, FSRA-regulated statutory benefits with different forms) and was not examined here. Recommend shared call engine and identity/consent layer with domain-specific scripts and data schemas (inference).

### Agent can execute versus staff
Agent can: status, payment trace, document list, adjuster contact, resubmission channel, authorization/night confirmation, schedule callbacks. Staff must: disputes, negotiation, supplements, depreciation release, coverage, anything touching liens, notices, deadlines as legal advice, any consent question the counterparty challenges.

### Five most important unknowns
1. Exact current text and effective dates of Ontario Construction Act prompt payment and lien rules, and their application to insurance-funded owner repairs (read e-Laws; ask Ontario construction counsel).
2. Whether Ontario insurers and adjusting firms will disclose claim and payment details to a contractor by phone on the strength of a direction to pay or work authorization, and what proof they require.
3. Real share of stalled invoices where phone is the bottleneck versus portal/email (needs customer invoice data).
4. Preferred-vendor and TPA program terms in Ontario (direct payment, pricing, AP channels) and whether any contractor-facing portals offer status a caller could use or must not circumvent.
5. Accommodation authorization practice: who authorizes, direct-bill rules, rate caps and extension approvals by insurer.
