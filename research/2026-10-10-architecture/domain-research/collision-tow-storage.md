## Part A. What I verified (or found in sources)

### A1. Repair and Storage Liens Act (RSLA), Ontario
Source: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90r25 (excerpts surfaced via search; versions /v6, /v8, /v9 listed), https://ontario.ca/laws/regulation/150427/v3 (O. Reg. 427/15), https://www.collisionrepairmag.com/new-regs-in-ontario-for-towing-and-storage-now-in-effect/ (trade press, paywalled)

- A repairer has a right to sell a lien-subject article after a 60-day period from when payment falls due (or from completion if no due date) (s. 3(3), as summarized). SECONDARY.
- A storer who received the article from someone other than the owner must give written notice of the lien to the owner and registered secured parties within 60 days of receipt (s. 4(4), as summarized). SECONDARY.
- O. Reg. 427/15, effective 2016-07-01: for vehicles with an Ontario Highway Traffic Act permit the notice period drops to 15 days after the day after receipt; if notice is late, the storer's lien is limited to the amount owing for the notice period. 60 days remains for vehicles without such a permit (for example out-of-province). Notice may go by fax or email in addition to personal delivery, registered mail, courier. SECONDARY (one trade summary plus the regulation listing; confirm exact text and current consolidation).
- s. 24 RSLA: a person "lawfully entitled" to the vehicle can pay the claimed amount into court and obtain an initial certificate; the lienholder must release within 3 days unless it files a notice of objection with the court (s. 24(6), as summarized). Forms listed in Reg. 1002 which the same source says was revoked 2018-03-20 by O. Reg. 110/18; current forms unchecked. SECONDARY. Sources: https://insurancelawblog.harpergrey.com/?p=17874 , https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/insurance/court-kept-insurers-auto-storage-costs-control-1004130818/
- Case: 2237446 Ontario Inc. (409 Collision Centre) v. Intact Insurance, 2018 ONCA 394 (Apr 24, 2018). Insured signed a storage agreement at $85 per day; insurer used the s. 24 certificate procedure; Court of Appeal held the insurer, which had assumed liability and was subrogated (Insurance Act s. 278), was a person "lawfully entitled" even before paying the insured; trade press says the court reasoned storage charges should not mount while issues are resolved. SECONDARY. Sources: https://ca.vlex.com/vid/2237446-ontario-inc-409-722175161 , https://vlex.com/vid/ontario-court-of-appeal-715668697
- Case: J.P. Towing v. Intact (Ont. Sup. Ct., 2019): Intact ordered to pay $70 per day storage on recovered vehicles, tied to a 2016 small claims ruling; a later report says the court found the tower overcharged in a dispute reaching $483K. SECONDARY (trade press only; outcomes of later stages not confirmed). Sources: https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/legal/how-intact-lost-this-battle-over-vehicle-storage-fees , https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/legal-insights/ontario-court-rules-towing-firm-overcharged-intact-as-dispute-hits-483k-574766.aspx
- Small claims example (Prozone Auto v. Mistri, 2025): legal owners used s. 24 and paid security into court to get vehicles released. SECONDARY. Source: https://ztgh.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/REASONS-and-DECISION-Mistry-Conjoined-Motions-SC-24-4694-Heard-January-28-2025-3.pdf

### A2. Towing and Storage Safety and Enforcement Act, 2021 (TSSEA) and regulations
Sources: https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/21t26/v3 , https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/230162/v6 (O. Reg. 162/23, charges), https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/230167/v4 (O. Reg. 167/23, general), https://www.ontario.ca/page/know-your-rights-when-getting-tow , https://www.ibc.ca/issues-and-advocacy/regulation/ontario-towing-storage-regulations

- Provincial regime in force 2024-01-01, replacing municipal bylaws; operators and storage facilities need a provincial certificate; company name and certificate number must appear on trucks, consent forms and invoices. SECONDARY. Sources: https://autosphere.ca/dealerships/2024/01/11/provincial-oversight-of-the-towing-industry-now-in-effect-in-ontario/ , https://insurance-canada.ca/2024/01/08/caa-ontario-oversight-towing-industry/
- Consent: Ontario's rights page says a tow needs the owner's consent unless initiated by police or an authorized official (e.g. illegal parking). SECONDARY (government page excerpt). Written consent form and a "Maximum Rate Schedule" are given before towing (IBC summary); O. Reg. 167/23 has consent and documentation-of-consent sections and a schedule of consent wording (sections reportedly 33 and 34 and a schedule; not read). SECONDARY.
- Rate disclosure: operators publish maximum rates for towing, storage and ancillary services and cannot charge above them; the Act does not itself fix fee amounts (Canadian Underwriter); maximum rates and certificate status are searchable on the government site. O. Reg. 162/23 defines "maximum rate schedule" and contains storage and "unreasonable amount" provisions (not read). SECONDARY. Source: https://canadianunderwriter.ca/news/claims/why-ontarios-new-tow-truck-regulations-arent-reducing-insurers-costs/
- Storage is charged on a prorated daily basis in one municipal FAQ example (partial first day prorated). SECONDARY, municipal illustration only. Source: https://www1.brampton.ca/EN/Business/Licensing/Documents/TSSEA%20FAQ%20February%202024.pdf
- Insurer role: per IBC's summary, a vehicle owner can authorize their insurer's representative to consent to towing services, access the vehicle and request release. SECONDARY (an owner-granted right, not a stated insurer duty).
- Enforcement/contact: Ministry of Transportation, towing@ontario.ca, 1-800-387-7736. SECONDARY.

### A3. Repair side
- FSCO text, quoted on a shop page: an insured may use the repair shop of their choice as long as the insurer approves the estimate; insurers may suggest preferred shops. SECONDARY (quoted on https://fixauto.com/mississauga-east/en/know-your-rights ; original FSCO page not seen; FSCO is now FSRA).
- DRP basics: insurers keep shop networks; insured is free to use a non-DRP shop (OTIP explainer). SECONDARY. Source: https://www.otip.com/Why-OTIP/News/Myth-buster-Repairing-your-car-after-a-collision
- Ontario Statutory Conditions: insurer may elect to repair/replace instead of paying cash if it gives written notice within 7 days of proof of loss. SECONDARY (search excerpt of https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/R03277).
- OPCF 49 (offered from 2024): optional endorsement under which a not-at-fault insured gives up recovery of repair costs and related items from their own insurer under direct compensation. Matters for who pays. SECONDARY. Sources: https://ibao.org/courses/2024-dcpd-changes-introduction-of-opcf-49 , https://www.collisionrepairmag.com/news/auto-insurance/article/15731080/questionable-bargain-opcf-49-agreement-offers-premium-discounts-for-drivers-who-forfeit-claim-compensation
- Platforms (vendor claims): CCC ONE offers DRP solutions: assignment routing, scheduling, access to insurer guidelines, rule-based estimate scoring, "Open Shop" for non-DRP shops to return estimates and photos electronically. UNVERIFIED for Canada/Ontario coverage and specific insurers. Sources: https://www.cccis.com/collision-repairers/drp-solutions , https://www.cccis.com/collision-repairers/estimating/drp
- A bookkeeping blog says CCC ONE, Mitchell and Solera Qapter (formerly Audatex) are the three estimating systems insurers use and that supplements go through the platform with insurer approval. UNVERIFIED (US-oriented blog). Source: https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/01/auto-body-collision-repair-shop-bookkeeping-drp-direct-repair-program-oem-aftermarket-lkq-parts-margins-ccc-mitchell-audatex-supplements-section-179-spray-booth-comeback-warranty-cycle-time-touch-time-severity-kpi-guide

### A4. Payment mechanics (all US/trade commentary, no Ontario source found)
- Shop-owner commentary: do not release vehicle until all billings including supplements are paid; use direction-to-pay only with trusted insurers and written assurance of the full supplement; some insurers ignore direction-of-pay on supplements; settlement payments sometimes come as bank drafts that can be cancelled after release; two-party cheques (insured and shop) are common where direct pay is not used. UNVERIFIED for Ontario. Sources: https://www.bodyshopbusiness.com/get-insurers-settle-payments-quickly/ , https://engineerfix.com/does-insurance-pay-the-body-shop-directly/

## Part B. Answers to the nine questions (with inference clearly marked)

### 1. Debtor, authorizer, payer, documents, DRP effect
What the sources support: the owner consents to the tow (A2) and signs storage agreements (409 Collision example, A1); the insurer can be authorized by the owner to consent to tows, access and release (IBC); the insurer may elect to repair (Statutory Conditions); the owner may choose any shop if the insurer approves the estimate (A3). Under direct compensation the insured's own insurer deals with the claim (inference from OPCF 49 material).

Inference (UNVERIFIED, test with shops):

| Charge | Legal debtor (contract) | Authorizes work | Usually pays | Documents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repair | Owner (shop's customer) | Owner signs repair authorization; insurer approves estimate | Insurer pays covered amount; owner pays deductible and non-covered | Repair authorization, estimate, possibly direction to pay |
| Supplement | Owner (same contract) | Owner plus insurer approval of the supplement | Insurer if approved | Supplement estimate, insurer approval record |
| Tow | Owner who signed consent (unless police-ordered, then rules differ, not verified) | Owner consent form, or owner-authorized insurer rep | Insurer typically reimburses under the policy, direct bill by arrangement | TSSEA consent form, max rate schedule, invoice |
| Storage | Owner under storage agreement/consent; insurer once it takes control or is authorized | Owner or authorized insurer | Insurer for the period it is responsible; disputed | Storage agreement, rate schedule, lien notice |
| Deductible | Owner | n/a | Owner, usually collected by shop at release | Invoice showing insurer portion and owner portion |

DRP effect: the sources do not describe DRP contract terms. Inference: DRP shops commonly have agreed rates and platform-based approvals, so the insurer becomes the expected payer and the shop's main counterparty, but the owner remains the contracting party at law. DRP agreements are private and could not be verified.

### 2. How status is learned today
Verified: nothing in Ontario sources. Vendor material shows estimating platforms (CCC ONE etc.) carry estimate and supplement submission to insurers electronically for shops that use them (UNVERIFIED for Ontario insurers). No verified insurer vendor portal for tow or storage invoices in Canada was found. Inference: estimate and supplement submission and approval is mostly electronic for DRP shops; invoice and payment status (remittance, cheque or EFT run, why a payment has not issued) and tow/storage invoices (usually not on estimating platforms) are phone or email. Phone is likely the real bottleneck for tow and storage operators and for payment trace; it is likely not the bottleneck for supplement approval at DRP shops. All UNVERIFIED; needs shop interviews.

### 3. Delay and non-payment reasons
All UNVERIFIED as a list (industry inference), except that storage disputes and lien procedures are supported by A1.

| Reason | Phone can resolve | Staff judgment | Legal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supplement not yet reviewed | Yes: status, ask to expedite | No | No |
| Supplement denied or partly approved | Get reason and what documents help | Yes: dispute | No |
| Appraisal or reinspection pending | Yes: schedule, assignee | No | No |
| Total-loss decision pending | Get status and expected date | Yes, if storage accruing | No |
| Storage authorization expired | Request extension or release instructions | Yes | Possibly (lien notice) |
| Rate dispute | Cannot settle alone | Yes | Maybe (Ontario max rate rules, small claims; J.P. Towing example) |
| Owner dispute | Not a call to the insurer | Yes | Maybe |
| Liability undecided | Get status | Yes | No |
| Police-ordered tow | Confirm who is paying | Yes | Unverified |
| Salvage | Confirm pickup and who owns | Yes | Lien/sale rules apply |

### 4. Rules and timelines
- RSLA: 60-day sale right for repairers; 15-day notice for Ontario-permit vehicles for storers (60 for others); s. 24 payment-into-court route with 3-day release; see A1. SECONDARY.
- TSSEA: consent, published maximum rates, certificates, no charging above published rates; see A2. SECONDARY.
- Insurer payment deadlines: **not found**. No Ontario source found for a fixed insurer deadline to pay supplements or invoices (FSRA search returned only temporary catastrophe measures). UNVERIFIED.
- Limits on storage fees to an insurer: published maximum rates (TSSEA), "unreasonable amount" provisions in O. Reg. 162/23 (not read), and court decisions (A1). No verified cap on what an insurer must reimburse, and no verified free storage period.
- Other provinces: not researched (no sources gathered). UNVERIFIED. Do not state any provincial notice deadlines.

### 5. Contact patterns
No verified sources. Inference: shops talk to the assigned appraiser or DRP coordinator; towers and storage operators reach the claims unit or the adjuster on the file; IVR and voicemail are common. Disclosure: the owner can authorize an insurer representative to deal with operators (IBC); the reverse (insurer disclosing to a shop) likely depends on owner authorization or a repair/tow authorization naming the shop. The legal basis under Canadian privacy law (PIPEDA/FIPPA-type rules for insurers) was not checked. UNVERIFIED.

### 6. Identifiers
Inference (UNVERIFIED), consistent with the tow documentation requirements in A2: claim number, policy number, insured name (owner), loss date, VIN or plate, adjuster or appraiser name, estimate and supplement ids, tow consent form number or PO, storage start date, daily rate (and posted max rate), operator certificate number (verified as printed on invoices, A2). Which identifier each insurer accepts is unknown.

### 7. Reconciliation
Nothing verified for Ontario. Commentary (A4) mentions two-party cheques, bank drafts and direction-to-pay being ignored. Inference: one insurer payment may cover repair plus tow plus storage net of deductible, with the shop paying a tow company from proceeds; remittance detail may be missing. Treat as hypotheses; this is where the product needs real shop remittance samples.

### 8. Accruing charges
Verified (SECONDARY): storage is a daily rate subject to published maximum, prorated in at least one municipal example; courts say charges should not mount while coverage issues are resolved (409 Collision); RSLA s. 24 lets an insurer stop accrual by paying into court. Unverified: who is liable for days after authorization ends (depends on the signed agreement and the insurer's authorization). Inference for cadence: files with accruing storage should be called more often and escalate faster than repair-only files, and every call should record the date storage started, the daily rate, and whether the insurer has authorized continued storage in writing.

### 9. Conclusion
Call objectives (inference):

- **Shop:** supplement status and approval; reinspection or appraisal scheduling; payment trace (issued? method? date? payee?); cheque reissue; resubmission channel; documents needed; direction-to-pay acknowledgement; total-loss status.
- **Tow:** invoice receipt and status; payment trace; approved rate acknowledgement; tow authorization or PO confirmation; who authorized (insurer or owner); police-ordered tow payer.
- **Storage:** continued storage authorization; release instructions; total-loss decision date; rate/days dispute; lien notice timing (staff-led); payment trace.

Workflow design (inference): one case-level object keyed by claim, with child invoices (repair, supplement, tow, storage, deductible) and per-invoice status, because several invoices share one claim and the same adjuster. Calls differ by business type, but the call script is the same skeleton with a different objective and identifiers. Separate workflow only for storage cadence and lien clocks.

Voice agent can execute: status checks, payment trace, ask for reissue or resubmission channel, document list, confirm authorization on file, capture promised dates, schedule callbacks. Hand to staff: rate disputes, denial arguments, owner disputes, liability, total-loss negotiation, any lien notice, sale or s. 24 decisions, anything legal.

Five most important unknowns:
1. Real shop and tow remittance practice: payee, method, remittance detail, and whether the insurer or the shop's customer pays tow and storage. (Interview 5 operators.)
2. Which Ontario insurers require what for disclosure to a shop/tower, and whether a repair or tow authorization is enough for an AI caller to discuss the file.
3. Exact current text of RSLA ss. 3, 4, 24, O. Reg. 427/15, O. Reg. 162/23 storage/unreasonable-amount sections, O. Reg. 167/23 consent sections (read in full; fetch failed here).
4. Whether any insurer or estimating platform in Canada offers API/portal status for supplements and invoices (to avoid a phone-only assumption for the wrong step).
5. Insurer rules limiting storage reimbursement (free days, caps, authorization expiry), which determine call cadence for accruing charges; also other provinces' lien rules (not researched).
