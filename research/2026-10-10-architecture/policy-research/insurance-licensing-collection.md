## 2. Success fee and contingency pricing (analysis, mostly UNVERIFIED)

- CDSA: contingent compensation is not itself prohibited in the sections read. Reg. 74 s. 19.1.1(1)(b) conditions one exemption on NOT being contingent, which implies contingency is ordinary for registered agencies. VERIFIED (text); inference is ours.
- If the debtor is a corporation (row 2), the CDSA does not apply, so fee design is a contract matter. UNVERIFIED for any further consumer protection overlay.
- SABS: assignment void (row 15), and s. 398 bans negotiating for compensation on a claimant's behalf. A percentage of an insurer payment for SABS invoices is therefore the highest risk fee. Safer: flat or per-call subscription fee (current model; src/billing/tiers.ts uses tiers and minutes, not percentages; success-fee code not found).
- CDSA s. 22(a) and (c) forbid collecting more than owed and extra payments from debtors for forbearance; only relevant on the individual-debtor path.
- Insurance Act s. 397 adjuster licence, and the definition's words "for compensation, directly or indirectly", mean fee-by-outcome on a claim settlement strengthens a licence argument. UNVERIFIED how FSRA treats pure payment follow-up.
- Quebec: contingency fees for non-lawyers, UNVERIFIED.

## 3. Human or signed authorization points (every one)

1. Tenant signs service agreement stating permitted purposes, non-individual debtors only, and PHIPA agent terms. (rows 5, 18)
2. Tenant (or its authorised signer) signs a letter of authority per payer, scoped to status inquiry. (row 20)
3. Patient/claimant written direction to pay for MVA invoices (statutory). (row 14)
4. Patient consent for PHI disclosure where tenant is a custodian and PHIPA does not otherwise permit. (row 21)
5. Human review before any call where the debtor may be an individual, sole proprietor, partner or guarantor, or the payer's legal entity type is unknown. (rows 1, 2)
6. Human decision before any denial, dispute, coverage interpretation or settlement discussion on an MVA or adjuster-adjacent claim (s. 397, s. 398). The agent must stop and hand off to licensed or tenant staff. (rows 9, 12)
7. Human (tenant staff) authorisation before threatening or stating legal action or sending to a lawyer; Reg. 74 s. 23(1) requires the creditor's written authority on the registered path. (row 6)
8. Human sign-off before any pricing that takes a percentage of recovered insurer payments. (section 2)

## 4. Questions for counsel

1. Does calling an insurer (a corporation) for a client's receivable fall within Reg. 74 s. 19.7, so that no CDSA registration is needed? What about reciprocal exchanges, Lloyd's, unincorporated payers, and Crown or government payers (WSIB, ADP)? Does s. 19.7 apply where the underlying debt is ultimately a policyholder's liability?
2. Does a status check, document-receipt confirmation, or payment-date inquiry amount to "investigates ... any such loss or claim" or "directly or indirectly solicits the right to negotiate" under the adjuster definition? Does the "agent of the property insured" exclusion help for a contractor's invoice?
3. Is a provider's own SABS invoice follow-up caught by Insurance Act s. 398 ("negotiate or attempt to negotiate for compensation ... on behalf of a claimant")? Is a provider a "claimant" in that sense?
4. May the product fee be a percentage of recovery without engaging ss. 397, 398, or SABS s. 62? Is a flat subscription safer in every vertical?
5. If a patient or policyholder call is ever allowed (including voice, which `insuranceOnlyPolicy.ts` does not mention), which CDSA obligations attach and can the tenant lawfully use us without registration (s. 24)?
6. What do FSRA guidance and Authority rules on unfair or deceptive acts and claims handling say about a third party calling on behalf of a provider? Does FSRA require written insured authorization for a provider's agent to discuss an SABS file?
7. Is our written client agreement enough to make us a PHIPA "agent", given cross-tenant learning?
8. CRTC unsolicited telecommunications rules and AI voice calls to businesses: applicable or not, and what is the 2026-132 outcome?
9. Are call recordings and AI disclosure at the start of calls required in Ontario for business-to-business calls (two-party versus one-party consent under the Criminal Code)?
10. Quebec, Alberta, BC: see section 5.

## 5. Other provinces (brief, all UNVERIFIED, from memory, nothing fetched yet)

- Quebec: Debt Collection Act / Act respecting the collection of certain debts (Loi sur le recouvrement de certaines créances) licensing is by the OPC for collection of debts owed by consumers; business debts reportedly outside. Adjusters are regulated under the Act respecting the distribution of financial products and services (AMF; claims adjusters). French-language rules apply to scripts.
- Alberta: Collection and Debt Repayment Practices Regulation and Fair Trading Act: collection agency licensing; business-debt scope not confirmed. Adjusters under the Insurance Act (Alberta) licensing; Alberta auto has a different benefit regime (no SABS).
- British Columbia: Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act, collection agency licensing for consumer debts; ICBC Part 7 benefits regime differs. Adjuster licensing under the Financial Institutions Act.
